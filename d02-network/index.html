    How it is computed

    The network has one input, one layer of H units and one output: P = c + Σj aj max(0, wj s + bj), with the rescaled stretch s = (λ − 1) / 6.6, which runs from 0 to 1 over the measurements. Every unit is a bent line (ReLU); the offset bj (often called bias) sets where it bends, the weight in wj how steeply it rises, the weight out aj how much of it is added. The output offset c is one more knob.

    Training minimises the mean squared error over all 25 measurements by plain gradient descent: every step moves every knob against its gradient, times the learning rate. The typical error shown is the root of the mean squared error (RMSE), in MPa. Training pauses by itself when 1000 more steps improve the typical error by less than 1 %, when every unit is switched off, or after 30 000 steps.

    The gradients come from an explicit backward pass (backpropagation, the chain rule): for one measurement with squared error L = (P̂ − P)2, ∂L/∂P̂ = 2(P̂ − P), ∂L/∂aj = ∂L/∂P̂ · hj, and for a unit that is switched on ∂L/∂wj = ∂L/∂P̂ · aj · s and ∂L/∂bj = ∂L/∂P̂ · aj; a switched-off unit passes nothing back. Step 3 shows these numbers for one measurement and updates with them (the offsets and the output offset are updated too, but not drawn). Step 2 averages them over all 25 measurements. The page checks the backward pass against finite differences.

    Random start (fixed by its number, so every run is repeatable): each unit opens to the right with a steepness wj between 2 and 3; unit j bends at a random place in the j-th of H equal slices of the stretch from 1 to about 8.6, so a unit may bend beyond the last measurement; the weights out start as random numbers with spread √(2/H), the output offset at 0. A unit that is switched off for every measurement gets no gradient and never learns, but it still bends the curve beyond the data.

    Step 1 uses a single bent line that is zero before the bend. The button walks downhill on the two knobs (the bend moves with four times the step of the slope) until the steps become tiny; this finds a nearby valley, which need not be the deepest one. The physics check computes the slope of the curve on every straight piece between stretch 1 and 7.6 and fails if any slope is negative.
